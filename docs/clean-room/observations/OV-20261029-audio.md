# OV-20261029-audio: how the original hands out its eight sound channels
Date: 2026-10-29
Status: confirmed (two rounds) and implemented
Observer: OpenVirtue maintainers (black-box probe, trace values)
Implementer: OpenVirtue maintainers

## Question
How do sounds take the eight channels, as a script sees them through `CHANNEL` and
`CHANNEL_0…7`? Which free channel does a sound take? Which busy one does it take when none is
free? When does a channel read free again? What do `STOP_SOUND`, volume 0, `SOUND_VOL` 0, an
emitter out of range and a flic do?

## Why It Matters
Retail scripts poll `CHANNEL_n` and `CHANNEL` to chain and limit sounds. OpenVirtue had these
behaviours:
- it gave −1 when all channels were busy;
- it freed channels whenever the host noticed a sound had ended;
- `STOP_SOUND` freed every channel at once, loops included;
- `CHANNEL` started at −1.

## Setup
- Emulator: native Linux DOSBox-X 2026.10.01 with `forcerate=60`. The carriers come from
  `ovtool oracle prepare audio` (`tools/OpenVirtue.Tools/AudioProbeCases.cs`).
- Sounds: `SyntheticWav` writes 8-bit mono tones of 1 s, 4 s and 0.25 s at 11025 Hz, and 1 s at
  22050 Hz.
- Sampler: a global `EACH_TICK.1` action prints a `C` line whenever the eight channel states
  change. The line holds `TICKS`, its own run count, `FLIC_FRAME`, `CHANNEL` and `CHANNEL_0…7`.
- Run with `SDL_VIDEODRIVER=dummy`. With a visible window the emulator dropped to two engine
  frames a second (`TIME_CORR` capped at 8), too coarse to time a sound. Without one it ran at
  about 60 cycles a second (0.25 ticks).

## Observations
| Case | Trace | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| one, rate-22, soundfile | channel 0 reads 1 in the playing cycle; free at `TICKS` +15.97 | a channel is busy for the sound's length in real time (16 ticks a second), 22 kHz included; `PLAY_SOUNDFILE` behaves the same |
| quarter | free at +4.19 | the same for 0.25 s |
| nine (one cycle) | `CHANNEL` 0…7, then the ninth gets 0 | the lowest free channel; when none is free, the oldest sound's channel |
| steal (one per cycle) | the ninth gets 0 and the tenth 1 | the oldest sound, not a fixed channel |
| reuse | after channel 0 frees, the next sound gets 0 (channel 1 busy) | lowest free, not round robin |
| same | the same sound twice: channels 0 and 1 | no restart |
| stop / stop-lag | after `STOP_SOUND` the channels read 1 for the rest of the cycle and the next one, and 0 from the cycle after; a sound played right after `STOP_SOUND` takes channel 2 and plays its full length | the stop takes two cycles to show |
| volume-0, sound-vol-0, far-emitter (`SDIST` 5, 25 away) | no channel taken; `CHANNEL` unchanged (0 at start, 1 after two sounds in round 2) | an inaudible sound is skipped and `CHANNEL` is not touched; volume 0.001 plays; the near-emitter control (`SDIST` 100) plays |
| flic | sound A on channel 0, the flic, then sound B on channel 1 at frame 10; both read free only in the first world cycle after the 120-frame flic | channel states do not change during a flic |
| sloop | the region's `SLOOP` ceiling sound holds channel 0 at 2 from before the first trace. Seven sounds take channels 1–7, and an eighth takes channel 1, the oldest normal sound | a `SLOOP` channel is never taken |
| stop-sloop | `STOP_SOUND` leaves channel 0 at 2 in every later cycle | `STOP_SOUND` does not stop a `SLOOP` loop |
| (all) | `CHANNEL` reads 0 before any sound | its initial value is 0 |

## Invariant
- **Taking a channel.** A sound takes the lowest free channel (`CHANNEL_n` 0). If none is free,
  it takes the channel whose normal sound started first. `SLOOP` channels read 2 and are never
  taken. `CHANNEL` becomes the channel taken, or −1 when every channel holds a loop (manual).
- **Skipped sounds.** A sound at volume 0, at `SOUND_VOL` 0, or from an emitter beyond its
  texture's `SDIST` takes no channel and leaves `CHANNEL` as it was.
- **When a channel reads free.** A channel reads busy from the instruction on, and free from the
  first world cycle after its sound has played out. During a flic the states do not change.
- **`STOP_SOUND`.** It stops the normal sounds. Their channels read free from the second cycle
  after it, and loops play on.

## Open Questions
- The exact end of a sound against `TICKS`. A 1-second sound read free 15.97 ticks after the cycle
  that started it, a quarter-second one 4.19 after, so the original's 16 ticks a second and the
  sound's real end do not line up exactly. The replay frees them a cycle late or early.
- Whether a `SLOOP` texture sound also gives way when its region is left. OpenVirtue's host
  stops it when it leaves earshot.
- Whether a stolen channel's old sound is cut at once. It cannot be observed through
  `CHANNEL_n`.
- How the floor and texture-cycle sounds (`SCYCLES`) take channels.
- Music (`PLAY_SONG`) and `PLAY_CD`, which use no channel.

## Implementation
- `WdlRuntime` does the channel bookkeeping:
  - `ReserveSoundChannel`: lowest free channel, else the oldest normal one; loops read 2.
  - `IsSoundAudible`: the skip rules.
  - `ReleaseFinishedChannels`: at the start of each world cycle, once its `TICKS` are set, it asks
    the host (`SoundPlayedOut`) which sounds have played out and frees their channels in that cycle.
    A quarter-second sound started at 13.438 reads free at 17.625, the first cycle after 17.438.
    Until 2026-10-30 the host noticed only after the cycle, a cycle late. `TickFlic` skips this.
  - `CompleteSound` still covers sounds the host stops itself.
  - `StopSounds`: one `StopChannel` request per normal channel, released two cycles later.
- `CHANNEL` now defaults to 0.
- The oracle-diff replay uses a timed audio sink that keeps each voice busy for its length in
  engine time. With it, every audio carrier replays with the original's channel states; the
  remaining differences are the harness's start-up `TICKS` offset.
- Tests: `WdlRuntimeTests` (`Runtime_SoundChannels_*`, `Runtime_CompletedSound_*`,
  `Runtime_StopSound_*`, `Runtime_InaudibleSound_*`).
