# OV-20261104 — Texture mapping: floors, ceilings, wall offsets and the repeat grid

Status: confirmed (two rounds, eight carriers) and implemented.

## Question
Every retail surface is a patterned texture, so where a texture's origin lies and which way its
columns and rows run decides whether a room looks like the original. The attach probe
(OV-20261104-attach) had read a wall's columns running from the second vertex and its rows
downward from each repeat's top on a wall standing on height 0. What of floors and ceilings,
what do a map's wall `OFFSET_X` and `OFFSET_Y` do (the mesh builder's sense was unprobed), and
do a wall's repeats stack from its region's floor or from height 0?

## Method
- Oracle: native Linux DOSBox-X 2026.10.01 with `SDL_VIDEODRIVER=dummy`, `forcerate=60`, the
  SaintsX-patched `VRUN.EXE` V3.963.
- Fixture generator: `ovtool oracle prepare texmap <dir>` (`tools/OpenVirtue.Tools/TexmapProbeCases.cs`).
  The texture is the level palette's 16×16 swatch (every index once) at `SCALE` 2 (8 steps a
  repeat) on walls, and a 64×64 bitmap of 4×4-pixel cells (cell c, r holding index 16r + c) at
  `SCALE` 4 (a cell a step, 16 steps a repeat) on the floor and ceiling, since the manual (p.41)
  wants floor and ceiling textures quadratic and 64×64 at least: round 1's 16×16 swatch drew
  as index 0 on the floor. Each screenshot pixel's index names the texel under it; the floor and
  ceiling points are mapped back through the measured projection (eye 8, `PLAYER_ARC` 1). The
  free wall stands 32 steps ahead from map y −4 to 4; the pad island (x −12…−4, y 12…20) is
  raised to floor 4 or sunk to −4 with the player turned to it (`PLAYER_ANGLE` 2.03, tilt 0.3
  for the pit). Traces (`OVTXM0.TXT`, two lines) hash `dce49209`.
- Confirmation that no proprietary code/assets are committed: synthetic level and bitmaps only;
  nothing under `_research/` is committed.

## Readings
| Carrier | Reading |
|---|---|
| floor (16×16 swatch) | index 0 over the whole floor: a 16×16 bitmap does not tile a floor |
| ceil (16×16 swatch) | a few indices of row 0 in no spatial order: the same |
| floor-cell | along the centre line (map y 0) the cell column is floor(−x) mod 16: 0 at x 47.1 and 31.8, 3 at 44.7, 15 at 32.9, 9 at 22.2; across a row at depth 32.9 the cell row is floor(y) mod 16: 0 at y 16.9, 14 at 14.6, 1 at 1.1, 14 at −1.1, 15 at −16.9 (facing +x, +y is to the left) |
| ceil-cell | the same grid as the floor |
| wall-plain | at the wall's screen-left end (the second vertex, map y 4) column 0, growing by one every half step toward the first vertex; down that end row 0 at the ceiling (16), 15 at 8.5, 0 again at 8, 15 just above the floor |
| wall-offset (`OFFSET_X 4; OFFSET_Y 6`) | column 4 at the second vertex; row 10 at the ceiling, row 0 at height 13, 15 at 9.5, 0 again at 5: the texture slid 4 pixels toward the origin and 6 pixels down |
| raised (pad floor 4, the step 0…4 seen from the room) | row 8 at the step's top (height 4) down to 15 at the floor |
| pit (pad floor −4, the far step 0…−4 seen over the edge) | row 0 at the step's top (height 0), rows 1…7 down to −4 |

- **A floor's or ceiling's texture columns run along −x and its rows along +y, both from the
  origin, at `SCALE` pixels a step**: texel column = floor(−x · SCALE_X) mod width, row =
  floor(y · SCALE_Y) mod height. Floor and ceiling textures must be 64×64 or larger squares.
- **A wall's texel column is (distance from the second vertex · SCALE_X + OFFSET_X) mod width;
  its row is (−height · SCALE_Y − OFFSET_Y) mod height**: rows run downward from the top of each
  repeat, and the repeats stack from height 0, not from the region's floor (row 8 at the top of
  a step up to 4, row 0 at the top of a step down from 0). `OFFSET_X` slides the texture toward
  the second vertex, `OFFSET_Y` slides it down.

## Open Questions
- The sense of a region's `FLOOR_OFFS_X/Y` and `CEIL_OFFS_X/Y`: answered by OV-20261104-blend
  (the X offset comes off the column, the Y offset adds to the row).
- Where a wall's attach origin lies on a wall whose floor is above 0: answered by
  OV-20261104-attach round 7 (the top of the height-0 grid's repeat above the floor) and corrected
  by OV-20261105-wall-pieces (a fixed 0 whatever the region's floor).
- Textures larger than 64×64 on surfaces, and `SCALE` values that are not whole pixels a step.

## Addendum (2026-11-05): a wall bitmap whose height is not a power of two
The manual (p.37) requires a wall bitmap's height to be a power of two and leaves the width free,
but the original reads the rows of any wall bitmap through `v & (H−1)`, the mask of the next power
of two, while the columns are a plain modulo of the width. A 12-row bitmap therefore spans 16 rows
in the order 0, 1, 2, 3, 0, 1, 2, 3, 8, 9, 10, 11, 8, 9, 10, 11, and retail's 120-row seat
curtain spans 128 (OV-20261105-wall-pieces). A wall's row is therefore (−height · `SCALE_Y` −
`OFFSET_Y`) taken modulo that period, and the period's row `r` shows the bitmap's row `r & (H−1)`.
OpenVirtue expands such a wall bitmap's rows at load time rather than changing the mapping; floors,
ceilings, sprites, attaches and UI keep their bitmap's real height.

## Round 3 (2026-11-10): where the walls end under a blank ceiling
`round3/blank-eye4`, `-eye8` and `-eye12` (in `_research/oracle-runs/OV-20261110-blank-ceiling`): the
16×16 swatch as the room's ceiling, which draws as entry 0, with the eye at 4, 8 and 12.

| Eye | The far wall (depth 48) in the screen's middle column |
|---|---|
| 4 | from row 23 down to row 294 |
| 8 | from row 23 down to row 349 |
| 12 | from row 23 down to row 404 |

- **Under a blank ceiling a wall's top stands a fixed height above the eye, not at the ceiling.** Its row
  stays at 23 whatever the eye, while the floor line moves 55 rows per 4 units (13.75 rows a unit at
  depth 48, the horizon at row 239). So the top is 15.7 to 15.9 above the eye: about the room's height,
  16, as if the ceiling were projected from the floor without the eye. With a 64×64 ceiling the same
  walls end at the ceiling, row 130 with the eye at 8, and OpenVirtue matches that.
- Not implemented. Of the retail levels only Heart has a blank ceiling, a 4×4 bitmap on its closed
  `border` regions, which never show a surface. Heights other than 16 and floors other than 0 are not
  measured.

## Implementation
`TextureLoader.BlankSurfaceTextures` names the textures a surface shows as entry 0, `MeshBuilder.Build`
takes them, and `RuntimeHost` applies it to a level read from an archive.
`MeshBuilder.SurfaceVertex` maps u to −x and v to +y at the texture's scale;
`EmitWallSegment` takes v as −height at the scale less `OFFSET_Y`, so the repeats stack from
height 0; `EmitWallAttachment` finds the first repeat's top on that grid. Tests:
`MeshBuilderTests` (`BuildWalls_AppliesWallTextureOffsetsInPixels`,
`BuildWalls_TextureRepeatsStackFromHeightZero`, `Build_AppliesRegionSurfaceTextureOffsetsInPixels`).

## Round 4 (2026-11-11): diagonal walls and off-grid corners
The retail shots of OV-20261110-retail-play round 7 differed on almost every wall texel. Three carriers asked
whether that came from wall angle or from corners off the grid:
- `round4/wall-diag45`: the swatch on a 45° wall from (24, −6) to (36, 6).
- `round4/wall-diag4`: a wall of slope 4 from (30, −8) to (34, 8).
- `round4/wall-frac`: the baseline wall with corners (32, −4.3) and (32, 4.61).

`OVTXS0.PCX` hashes (sha256, first 8): wall-diag45 `47673fa6`, wall-diag4 `b5ea1fed`, wall-frac
`2fd78bb2`.

OpenVirtue's frames differ by 2398, 2467 and 1749 pixels. That is the size of the baseline
`wall-plain`'s difference (1542) and `wall-offset`'s (1427): rows along the wall's edges, not shifted
columns. **A diagonal wall's columns run along the straight-line distance from the second vertex, and an
off-grid corner gives nothing new.** No change. The retail shots' texel differences came from the replay
clock, not from the mapping (retail-play round 7).

Open after round 4: on the two diagonal walls the original's texel columns lead OpenVirtue's by about 0.85 of
a screen column. For wall-diag45, fitting the exact geometric u to its swatch band leaves 1 of 188
columns off with the sample taken 0.85 column further along than OpenVirtue's. Shifting the whole picture
that way makes every probe worse: five rendering probes went from 1.92 to 2.55 million differing pixels.
Shifting only the walls' u by 0.85 · dFdx(u) brings wall-diag45 from 2398 to 862 and wall-diag4 from 2467
to 1256, but the facing walls get worse (wall-plain 1542 to 2354). So the lead grows with the wall's angle
to the line of sight. A hit point displaced along the ray before u is taken would behave like that; it is
not fitted. Nothing changed.
A refit of the original's frames alone, using index mod 16 on every seventh row from 150 to 329 over the
columns each wall covers, sorts the carriers by wall direction, not by angle. Both walls along an axis take
the exact u at the column's own ray (δ −0.1 to 0 of a column; wall-frac: 0 misses in 4082). Both oblique walls,
45° and slope 4, take it 1.75 to 1.85 columns further along (52 of 6214 and 78 of 7488 off). Moving the hit
along the ray, by a ratio or by a distance, fits neither pair. So u seems to be computed one way for walls along
an axis and another way for the rest.

## Note (2026-11-12): the retail floors' sideways offset is not a half texel
In the retail shots (OV-20261110-retail-play, Legalism's first), the floor rows are best matched by shifting OpenVirtue's
picture right by 0 pixels near the horizon, growing to about 1 pixel at rows 320 to 400. That shape is a sideways offset
fixed in the world, about 1/32 unit at row 400, not a screen shift and not an eye-height error (which would show most near
the horizon). The synthetic floor carrier matches to 452 pixels. Offsetting floor and ceiling texture coordinates by
±0.5 texel in either axis made Legalism's and Apathy's shots and the floor carrier worse: 1.29 million differing pixels
became 1.55 to 1.74 million. The cause is open. Candidates are the player's sideways position in the frame (its 22.10
rounding) and how far the retail floors lie from the map's origin. The ceil carrier's 16×16 swatch is a size the manual
rules out for ceilings, and its picture is not followed.

## Round 8 (2026-11-12): a retail floor bitmap, eye heights, and a floor's column
Four carriers hand-made from round7/floor-turned (`round8/`, `OVTXS0.PCX`: eye-a0 `d4d8da2b`, eye-turned `431a65f2`, leg-a0 `8eb4825e`, leg-e8 `18864a59`):
- eye-a0 and eye-turned: the swatch floor with the eye at Legalism's 4.594 instead of 8, headings 0 and 1.899.
- leg-a0 and leg-e8: Legalism's own maze floor bitmap (the user's file, copied into the local carrier only; 256 x 256 at
  `SCALE_XY 32`), heading 0, eyes 4.594 and 8.

Readings:
- **The eye height plays no part.** eye-a0 and eye-turned differ by 456 and 77 pixels, along the edges.
- **The original draws a bitmap's raw indices through the palette shown, even with no `RANGE`.** In the probe palette
  Legalism's floor came out as speckles of probe colours. OpenVirtue, whose indexed path needed a `RANGE`, drew the
  bitmap's own colours. With no `RANGE` it now draws by index with the identity shade table
  (`RuntimeHost.UploadShading`). leg-a0 went from 112 843 to 30 385 pixels and leg-e8 from 83 265 to 24 615. No other
  rendering screen changed.
- **A floor pixel shows the texel of the screen column one to its left.** On both leg carriers, every floor row band and
  every 64-column band matched best with OpenVirtue's picture moved one column right. The swatch's 4-pixel cells hid
  this, except along their edges. Ceilings showed no such shift: with it, the retail shots got worse. Implemented in the
  GL fragment shader for surfaces below the eye. leg-a0 went to 13 455 and leg-e8 to 15 475. Over the texmap,
  wall-pieces, shade-global, shading, sprite-shade, blend, ui-widgets, clip-view and retail-play screens, 4 273 615
  differing pixels became 4 102 376, with 21 screens better and 4 slightly worse (retail shots after their walks have
  drifted). A shift of 1.25 columns fits the sum a little better (texel edges), but the band fits say one column.
